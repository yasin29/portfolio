## At a glance

Futurenation is a national skills-and-employment platform for Bangladesh, delivered through a public–private partnership involving UNDP and the country's ICT Division alongside private-sector partners.

I ran it as Project Manager from the offshore delivery side — sprint planning, stakeholder reporting, requirements, UAT, and release.

A national employment platform has a stakeholder problem before it has a technical one. A government department, a UN agency, and private-sector partners each arrive with a legitimate and different definition of success: policy outcomes, development targets, and commercial viability respectively.

None of them is wrong, and a delivery team cannot optimise for all three simultaneously without someone deciding what the trade-offs are. On a project of this shape, the PM work is largely making those trade-offs explicit rather than letting them surface as late-stage disagreement.

## My role

Project Manager on the offshore delivery team:

- **Sprint planning and standups** across the engineering team.
- **Stakeholder reporting** to institutional and private-sector partners on a fixed cadence.
- **Requirements documentation** — PRD, BRD, and SRS.
- **UAT and release management** through to live.

## Approach

**Report on a cadence, not on demand.** With multiple institutional stakeholders, ad-hoc reporting means the loudest partner gets the most information. A fixed reporting rhythm meant everyone saw the same picture at the same time, which is what stops a project of this kind fragmenting into parallel conversations.

**Write requirements for people who did not attend the meeting.** In a partnership this broad, the person approving a requirement is often not the person who discussed it. That raises the bar on documentation — PRD, BRD, and SRS had to stand on their own, not act as reminders of a conversation.

**Treat UAT as the alignment checkpoint.** For a platform serving a public mandate, acceptance testing is where stated requirements meet what stakeholders actually expected. Running UAT properly, rather than as a formality before release, is what caught the gaps between those two things while they were still fixable.

## What shipped

- A live national skills-and-employment platform at [platform.futurenation.gov.bd](https://platform.futurenation.gov.bd/).
- Requirements documentation across PRD, BRD, and SRS.
- A UAT and release process the institutional partners signed off against.

## Outcome

- Live and operating as national infrastructure, with UNDP and the ICT Division among the partners.
- Delivered offshore, across a cross-border team and a multi-party stakeholder group.
- One of the engagements that established how I handle stakeholder complexity: fixed cadence, written requirements, and UAT as a real checkpoint.

## What I'd do differently

Agree a single success metric with all partners at kickoff. Each stakeholder had their own definition, and while the reporting cadence kept everyone informed, it did not force the harder conversation about which outcome wins when two of them conflict. Naming that early is uncomfortable and cheaper than discovering it during acceptance.
