## At a glance

Ivorymade is a production-lab and management platform for dental restorations, serving US and Canadian dental labs — order intake, 3D restoration-model review, payments, and referrals. I ran it as Project Manager across two delivery phases with a team of five, on the legacy three-month delivery model.

Phase 1 took the platform to an MVP where every order was functional end to end. Phase 2 built on that with role-based access, multiple payment terms, and multi-office support.

## The problem

  src="/case-studies/ivory/feature3.webp"

A dental lab's work is a chain of handoffs: a dentist submits a case, a technician designs the restoration, someone reviews the 3D model, it gets produced, and it gets invoiced. Before the platform, that chain lived across email, phone calls, and files, so the status of any given order depended on whoever last touched it.

The build had to make an order the unit of truth — and it had to do that before adding the complexity of who is allowed to do what, how each lab prefers to pay, and which office a case belongs to. Getting that sequencing wrong would have meant modelling permissions and billing on top of an order flow that was not yet stable.

## My role

Project Manager end-to-end across both phases, managing five team members. I owned the process from kickoff to handover rather than a slice of it:

- **Requirements and specification** — requirement gathering, user stories, PRD and SRS.
- **Technical and design direction** — technical decisions, design requirements and guidelines, and the UI/UX feedback loop with the designers.
- **Delivery process** — Agile/Scrum with daily standups, sprint planning, sprint review, and retrospectives.
- **Client communication** — weekly client meetings and daily written updates.
- **Quality** — wrote the test cases and ran the SQA pass myself.
- **Release** — deployment plan and handover documentation through to delivery.

## Approach

**Phase 1 — make every order work.** The goal was a genuine MVP, not a demo: a case could be submitted, designed, reviewed against the 3D model, produced, and invoiced without leaving the platform. Anything that was not required to make an order functional was deliberately deferred, which is what kept a five-person team on a three-month cycle.

**Phase 2 — make it work for a real organisation.** Once the order flow was stable, the platform grew into how labs are actually structured: role-based access so technicians, reviewers, and administrators see different things; multiple payment terms rather than a single billing assumption; and multi-office support so a group operating several locations could keep cases separated.

Running both phases the same way mattered. Each started at kickoff with its own user stories, PRD, and SRS, rather than treating Phase 2 as a change request bolted onto Phase 1.

I owned the design loop directly — writing the design requirements and guidelines, reviewing screens, and giving feedback until they matched the specification. Reading the code meant I could debate technical decisions with the engineers instead of relaying them.

- **Phase 1:** order intake and dashboard, 3D restoration-model review through the exocad viewer, production tracking, invoicing, and reports — every order functional end to end.

- Full delivery artefacts alongside the product: PRD, SRS, test cases, deployment plan, and handover documentation.

## Outcome

- Live with US and Canadian dental labs, generating **$138K in US and Canada sales in the first half of 2026**.
- Both phases delivered by a team of five on the legacy three-month model, with the phase split holding — Phase 2 extended the order flow rather than reworking it.
- The documentation structure I built here — PRD, SRS, and test cases in one place — became the template I reused on Artlive and Bside.

## What I'd do differently

Model the permissions and payment terms during Phase 1, even though they were not built until Phase 2. Deferring the *build* was right; deferring the *thinking* meant Phase 2 opened with data-model questions that would have been cheaper to answer while the schema was still soft.

I would also have started the AI-native delivery approach here. Ivorymade ran on the legacy three-month model; Artlive, a larger build, shipped afterwards with three people because the workflow had changed. The contrast is instructive, but Ivorymade paid the older price.
