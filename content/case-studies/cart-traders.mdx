## At a glance

Cart Traders is a mobile marketplace for trading cards — listing, discovery, and transactions between collectors, on iOS and Android.

I ran it as Project Manager from kickoff to handover with a team of three, on the legacy delivery model.

## The problem

<Figure
  src="/case-studies/cart-traders/screen-1.webp"
  alt="Cart Traders mobile app showing the trading-card marketplace feed"
  caption="Condition is the price - discovery has to make it legible at a glance."
/>

Collectible marketplaces live or die on two things: whether a listing accurately represents the item, and whether both sides trust the transaction. Trading cards make both harder than usual, because condition is the price — the same card in two grades is effectively two different products.

The build had to make condition and authenticity legible inside a mobile listing flow that stays fast enough for someone photographing a stack of cards. Get the listing flow wrong and there is no marketplace, because there is no inventory.

## My role

Project Manager end-to-end, managing three team members:

- **Requirements and specification** — requirement gathering, user stories, PRD and SRS.
- **Technical and design direction** — technical decisions, design requirements and guidelines, and the UI/UX review loop.
- **Delivery process** — Agile/Scrum with daily standups, sprint planning, sprint review, and retrospectives.
- **Client communication** — weekly client meetings and daily written updates.
- **Quality** — wrote the test cases and ran the SQA pass.
- **Release** — deployment plan, app-store submission, and handover documentation.

## Approach

<Figure
  src="/case-studies/cart-traders/screen-3.webp"
  alt="Cart Traders listing flow capturing card details and structured condition"
  caption="Camera-first listing with condition captured as structured data."
/>

**Optimise the listing flow above everything.** In a two-sided market the supply side is the bottleneck at launch. I specified the listing path as the highest-priority experience — fewest steps, camera-first, condition captured as structured data rather than free text.

**Structure condition, don't describe it.** Free-text condition notes are unsearchable and unenforceable. Making grade a structured field was the specification decision that let discovery, pricing, and dispute handling all work off the same source.

**Legacy model, documented the same way.** Three people on the three-month cycle, with the full artefact set — PRD, SRS, test cases, deployment plan, handover — so the client owns the system after delivery rather than depending on the build team.

- Delivered and handed over with complete documentation.

- Delivered with three people on the legacy three-month model.

## What I'd do differently

Specify the dispute path at the same time as the happy path. We built listing, discovery, and transaction thoroughly; what happens when a buyer disagrees with a stated grade is exactly the scenario that defines trust in a collectibles marketplace, and it deserved the same specification attention as the flows that go right.
