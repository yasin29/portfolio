## At a glance

Artlive is a two-sided marketplace connecting art collectors with certified appraisal experts — three distinct service flows, a Stripe Connect and PayPal payment architecture, and expert discovery at the centre of the experience.

I ran it as Project Manager from kickoff to handover with a team of three. It is a larger build than Ivorymade delivered by a smaller team, because this was the first project I ran on the AI-native delivery approach rather than the legacy three-month model.

## The problem

<Figure
  src="/case-studies/artlive/screen-1.webp"
  alt="Artlive landing experience — connect with certified art experts and choose a service"
  caption="Discovery leads with certified experts and an explicit service choice."
/>

Art appraisal is a trust transaction before it is a commercial one. A collector handing over a valuation request needs to believe the expert is genuinely qualified; the expert needs to be paid reliably without chasing invoices. A marketplace that gets either half wrong does not get a second attempt.

The structural challenge was that "an appraisal" is not one thing. Different service types carry different scopes, turnaround expectations, and price structures — so the payment architecture could not assume a single transaction shape. Money had to split correctly between platform and expert, across two providers, for every flow.

## My role

Project Manager end-to-end, managing three team members:

- **Requirements and specification** — requirement gathering, user stories, PRD and SRS.
- **Technical and design direction** — technical decisions, design requirements and guidelines, and the UI/UX review loop.
- **Delivery process** — Agile/Scrum with daily standups, sprint planning, sprint review, and retrospectives.
- **Client communication** — weekly client meetings and daily written updates.
- **Quality** — wrote the test cases and ran the SQA pass.
- **Release** — deployment plan and handover documentation through to delivery.

## Approach

<Figure
  src="/case-studies/artlive/screen-3.webp"
  alt="Artlive expert profile showing online valuation, buyer support, and bespoke service pricing tiers"
  caption="Each of the three service flows carries its own scope, turnaround, and price shape."
/>

**Model the money first.** Payments were the constraint everything else had to fit, so the payment architecture was settled before feature work began. Stripe Connect handles the split between platform and expert; PayPal covers collectors who will not use anything else. Deciding this at specification time rather than mid-build is what kept three service flows from becoming three separate payment implementations.

**Make expertise legible.** Discovery leads with credentials, specialisms, and previously reviewed objects, because in this market the expert profile is the product. That shaped the design guidelines I wrote for the team.

**AI-native delivery.** This is where the approach changed. Workflows built in Claude Code, n8n, and Notion absorbed the documentation drafting, ticket scaffolding, and status-reporting overhead that previously consumed a large share of PM time. The result was a bigger scope than Ivorymade shipped with three people instead of five — the same process, run faster, without dropping standups, reviews, retrospectives, or the SQA pass.

## Outcome

- Live at [artlive.com](https://www.artlive.com/), with collectors and certified experts transacting on the platform.
- Delivered with **three people on a scope larger than a five-person project** — the clearest evidence I have that the AI-native workflow is a multiplier rather than a label.
- The documentation template established on Ivorymade carried over directly, so specification time went into the hard parts instead of the format.

## What I'd do differently

Pilot one service flow end-to-end before specifying all three. We designed the flows in parallel, which was efficient on paper but meant assumptions about pricing and turnaround were validated late. Shipping one flow first would have tested the payment model against real behaviour while the other two were still cheap to change.
